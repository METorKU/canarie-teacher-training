## Band 8 model answer
In many countries, young adults are leaving rural communities to live in cities. This movement is mainly driven by better employment and educational opportunities, but it can create serious difficulties for both rural and urban areas.

The most important cause is the difference in job prospects. Cities usually contain a larger number of businesses, factories and service-sector employers, so young people can find work that is better paid and more varied than farming or small local jobs. Education is another major factor. Universities, vocational colleges and specialist training centres are often located in urban areas, and young adults may move in order to gain qualifications and improve their future careers. Cities also offer shops, cultural events and social opportunities that may be limited in the countryside.

However, this trend can harm rural communities. When working-age residents leave, villages may have fewer people to run farms, local businesses and essential services. The population can become older, making it harder for these areas to remain economically active. At the same time, rapid migration places pressure on cities and can widen inequality between prosperous urban districts and neglected rural regions. Demand for housing may push rents and property prices up, while roads, public transport and hospitals can become overcrowded.

In conclusion, young people move to cities primarily for work, study and lifestyle opportunities. Although this can improve their individual prospects, governments need to invest in rural employment and urban infrastructure to limit the negative effects. Better regional planning could spread opportunity more evenly nationwide.
