## Band 8 model answer
It is often argued that governments should give priority to public transport rather than constructing additional roads. I largely agree, particularly in cities, because reliable mass transit can reduce congestion and pollution. Nevertheless, road investment remains necessary in some areas.

The strongest reason to prioritise public transport is that it uses limited urban space far more efficiently than private cars. A single train or bus can carry hundreds of passengers, whereas the same number of motorists would occupy a large stretch of road and spend longer in traffic. In cities such as Bangkok, extending rail lines and improving bus connections would give commuters a practical alternative to driving. This would not only shorten journeys but also make access to work and education more affordable for people who cannot own a car.

Public transport also brings substantial environmental benefits. Road building can encourage additional car use, while well-run rail and bus systems reduce the number of vehicles producing exhaust fumes. Fewer cars on the road would improve air quality and help cities meet climate targets. These gains are especially important for children, older people and residents who live near busy roads.

That said, governments should not abandon road spending altogether. Rural communities need safe roads for deliveries, emergency services and connections to nearby towns. Freight routes also support the economy. However, this investment should be targeted at maintenance and essential links rather than creating more urban road capacity.

In conclusion, public transport deserves the larger share of funding in congested cities because it is more efficient, inclusive and environmentally responsible. New roads should still be built where they are genuinely needed, especially outside major urban areas.
