## Band 8 model answer
Working from home has become increasingly common. Although this arrangement can create isolation and make it harder to separate work from private life, I believe its advantages outweigh its disadvantages when employers manage it responsibly.

The principal benefit is the removal of the daily commute. In large cities, employees may spend several hours each week travelling to and from an office. Working remotely gives them more time for family, exercise and rest, which can improve wellbeing and reduce burnout. It may also let employers recruit talented staff regardless of where they live. It also broadens access to employment. Parents, people with disabilities and residents of smaller towns can apply for roles that would otherwise be impractical because of distance. At a wider level, fewer commuting journeys can reduce congestion and transport emissions.

There are, however, genuine disadvantages. Employees who work entirely from home may miss informal conversations with colleagues, and new staff can find it harder to learn through observation. In addition, the presence of work devices in the home can encourage people to answer messages outside normal hours. These risks should not be dismissed, but they can be reduced. Hybrid schedules allow teams to meet regularly, while mentoring programmes and explicit policies on availability can protect both development and work-life balance.

In conclusion, remote work has drawbacks, especially for collaboration and boundaries. Nevertheless, the savings in time, the broader access to jobs and the environmental benefits are more significant. With hybrid working and clear management practices, the disadvantages are manageable.
